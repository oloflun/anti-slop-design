# Taste Profile: Florhuset Majorna

Built blind, one-shot, from three references described in words only (no screenshots
taken, no pixels studied). Two references are catalog entries; `klova/entry.md` named in
the task does not exist on disk (the Klova reference lives only as a site export plus
README, no catalog `entry.md`), so the fallback rule applies: "if present, else any two."
The two present catalog entries actually read in full are Calyx and Tidvatten. The third
reference is the brief's own world.

## Existing Design System

None. Florhuset Majorna has no prior site, no tokens, no logo file. This is Tier 3
(invention) inside the design skill's gate, guided by the taste profile below rather than
by a screenshot to study. Nothing to inherit; everything below is a deliberate choice.

## Reference Analysis

### Reference 1 — Calyx (catalog entry, cream botanical atelier)

**What makes this feel good:**
- One serif (Cardo) carries every voice, roman for body and hero, italic for headline
  accents and product names, so the reader learns the code in a single screen.
- A single tracked-out mono (PT Mono, capped at 11px / 0.22em) does every label and never
  grows past caption size. Serif speaks, mono records; the split is absolute.
- The economic centre is a ruled, priced index, not a hero claim. Naming a real product
  and a real number does more persuading than an adjective ever could.
- Exactly one accent (rust) appears only at display scale, drop caps and one title, never
  as fill on a small control.

**Craft details worth noting:**
- 12-column grid used asymmetrically: labels in columns 1-3, content in 4-12, the empty
  left column read as a luxury signal rather than filled with clutter.
- Every photograph is captioned like an archive entry (commission number, location, a
  `NN / total` counter), which turns a stock-feeling image into evidence of real work.
- One tonal inversion at the very close: a single dark panel with nothing on it but the
  contact address, so the CTA feels like a different room, not a louder button.

**What to borrow (principle, not pixel):**
- Price the work in public — a ruled list of named items with a spec line and a real
  number sells harder than a hero headline.
- One accent, reserved for exactly the moments that need to carry weight.
- End the page on one inversion, not a repeated gradient footer.

**What to leave:** the fixed full-page grain overlay (a real render cost for a texture
almost no one consciously registers); the blanket reveal applied to every heading, list
item and figure alike, which flattens emphasis rather than creating it; copy that leans on
its own poeticism more than once per page ("smells like a memory") reads as costume by the
third repetition.

### Reference 2 — Tidvatten (catalog entry, cold-slate bathhouse ledger)

**What makes this feel good:**
- Three faces, three sealed jobs, with zero overlap: a grotesk shouts headlines and
  prices, a serif carries every sentence of prose, a mono (capped at 10.5px/0.22em) does
  every label and nothing else. A reader can tell a fact from a voice from a label by
  shape alone, with no chrome needed to signal which is which.
- The ground itself is the argument: a specific cold slate, not a generic off-white, so
  every warm colour on the page is rationed against it and means something when it
  appears.
- Persuasion runs in administrative register: the page quotes its own bylaws and opening
  hours instead of asserting benefits. A numbered rule is more credible than an adjective
  because it costs the writer something to be wrong about it.

**Craft details worth noting:**
- A sticky label column (kicker + head, columns 1-3) pinned while the reading column
  scrolls in 4-12 keeps the reader oriented without a nav bar.
- The signature move drives two numbers together from opposite ends of a hairline,
  scrubbed continuously off scroll position in both directions, not a one-shot trigger.
- Every muted tone was measured on both grounds it appears on; a caption grey that reads
  fine on a light section can go invisible on a dark one if it isn't re-picked per ground.

**What to borrow (principle, not pixel):** commit to a specific, describable ground colour
rather than a generic neutral; sell through your own real operating details (hours, a
capacity number, a cancellation policy) rather than through claimed benefits; keep the
label typeface capped at caption size for its entire life on the page, never let it grow
into a sentence.

**What to leave:** the fixed grain overlay again (both references reach for it and both
write, in their own critique sections, that it costs more than it delivers); the "no
platinum" values line, which Tidvatten's own reference lock flags as a too-close paraphrase
of Hyperborea's "no platinum" line, a reminder to borrow the *move* (a stated refusal) and
never translate the actual sentence; a middle third that runs two consecutive near-empty
viewports of restraint, which reads as unfinished rather than confident the second time.

### Reference 3 — the brief's own world (Florhuset Majorna)

**What the brief itself supplies as taste signal, read closely rather than skimmed:**
- "Ingen premium-jargong" is an anti-reference stated directly: whatever this becomes, it
  must not sound like a luxury concierge service. Warmth over polish.
- "Hantverk, säsong, väster-havet" names three forces at once: this is a maker's shop
  (binderi evenings are literally taught here), it changes with the calendar (no evergreen
  stock photo bouquet), and it sits on the Gothenburg archipelago side of the city, not
  the inland one. Majorna is a specific, slightly salt-worn, working-class-turned-mixed
  neighbourhood, not a boutique district.
- A named street address (Mariaplan 4, 414 70 Göteborg) and a real job for the page
  (order a bouquet, book a wedding meeting) push this toward Tidvatten's administrative
  register (real hours, real terms) more than toward Calyx's rarer, more precious
  commission-counter scarcity. Florhuset takes weekly orders and runs a subscription; it is
  not eleven commissions a week.

**Emotional target, drawn from all three:** the confident, unshowy competence of someone
who has done this every week for years and is not trying to impress anyone, warmed by
the specific texture of one Gothenburg neighbourhood and one changing season, never
dressed up as a concierge brand.

## Emotional Target

Should feel like walking past an actual flower shop with the door propped open on a cold,
bright Majorna morning: practical, a little salt-worn, obviously the work of someone's
hands, priced honestly on a chalkboard rather than a laminated menu. Not hushed-gallery
precious (that is Calyx's register, and this business takes weekly orders, not eleven
commissions), not spa-calm (that is Tidvatten's register). Warm competence, not luxury.

## Aesthetic Principles

1. **Cold ground, warm one bloom.** The paper is a granite-and-moss cool stone, not cream,
   so that the single saturated accent (a living poppy-red, not a muted print rust) reads
   as an actual flower against a Gothenburg autumn sky rather than as ink on a page.
   _Test: does the accent only ever appear on something that could plausibly be a live
   flower, a price, or a drop numeral, never as a filled button or a background tint?_
2. **Sell through the operation, not the adjective.** Every claim on the page is either a
   named product with a price, a real recurring time (second Thursday, Friday delivery),
   or a real address and hours. No "handgjord kärlek i varje bukett" (invented benefit
   language) anywhere.
   _Test: could the sentence be replaced with "trust us, we're great" without losing
   information? If yes, cut it._
3. **One voice, one record, no third.** A single warm serif (Fraunces, chosen for a softer
   contemporary hand than Cardo's old-style formality, since Florhuset is a shop front,
   not a print house) carries every headline and every sentence of prose; a single mono
   (IBM Plex Mono, capped at the house 10.5px/0.22em kicker size) carries every label,
   price qualifier and date, and never grows past that size.
   _Test: does any label ever get large enough to read as a sentence, or any sentence ever
   set in mono?_
4. **Ledger where the content is a list.** Seasonal bouquets and subscription tiers are
   typeset rows with a price right-aligned in display serif, not icon cards.
   _Test: would this section survive with the icons deleted? If the icons were load-bearing,
   it was a card grid pretending to be content._
5. **Register through operating detail, not spa hush.** Where Tidvatten quotes bylaws,
   Florhuset quotes its own real terms: opening hours, subscription cancellation notice,
   workshop seat count. The persuasion is administrative, not evocative.
   _Test: does the sentence resolve to a number, a day, or an address? If it resolves to a
   mood word instead, revise it._

## Quality Level

Production. Every token named and reused, every image either a verified real photograph
or an honestly labelled placeholder, all states and breakpoints considered, but this is a
neighbourhood florist's site, not a flagship gallery piece: restraint over spectacle,
readable over precious.

## Anti-References

| Anti-reference | What makes it wrong for us |
|---|---|
| A generic "premium florist" template (soft pink gradient hero, script logo, "luxury blooms" copy) | This is exactly the premium-jargong the brief rules out by name. It also fails the generic-default self-test: any competent default generator reaches for this first. |

## Craft Standards

- **Shadows:** none. Hierarchy comes from scale and the hairline system, not elevation.
- **Borders:** 1px hairlines at roughly 15-20% opacity of ink-on-paper or paper-on-ink,
  never a solid heavy rule except the single hairline under each kicker.
- **Radius:** sharp corners on the ledger and photo plates (this is a catalogue, not a
  hospitality brand); one soft exception, a 2px radius on the typeset CTA underline
  hit-area only, invisible until focus/hover.
- **Colour usage:** 9 named tokens, cool granite-moss paper family plus one saturated
  poppy accent used only at display scale (drop numerals, one price emphasis, the hero
  kicker rule). Every muted tone (stone/moss-mid) gets a second value defined for the dark
  ground panel, never reused unchanged across the inversion.
- **Typography pairing:** Fraunces (display and body, roman + italic mixed inside the
  hero line only) with IBM Plex Mono (kicker/label/price-qualifier, capped 10.5px/0.22em).
  Two families, hard roles, no third face introduced anywhere on the page.
- **Whitespace:** generous but not hushed-gallery empty; enough air around the ledger rows
  to read as considered, not so much that a working shop's site feels unstaffed.
- **Animation:** calm, not springy. One shared reveal (translateY 28px, ~1.2s,
  cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)) that fails toward visible without JavaScript, plus one modest
  two-layer scroll scrub on the hero (photo drift + headline resolve) as the page's single
  signature move, not a chain of triggered effects down the page.

## Personality

If Florhuset Majorna were a person: someone in a canvas apron who has bound bouquets in
the same shop for eleven years, knows every regular's order by the second sentence,
answers "how much" before you finish asking, and would rather tell you the delphinium
sold out this week than pad the order with something worse. Direct, warm, unbothered by
whether you know the Latin names. Dresses practically. Speaks in short, complete sentences
that resolve to a price or a day, not a mood.

## Applied lane and register (for the build)

- **Lane (one phrase):** Cold granite paper and one poppy-red bloom, catalogued like a
  harbour chandlery's stock list rather than a boutique's brochure.
- **Register:** Editorial print (licensed for florist/atelier per the register table),
  with two deliberate departures from the print-register AI-default (warm cream + serif
  display + terracotta accent): (1) palette temperature runs cool granite-moss instead of
  warm cream, and (2) the type pairing is Fraunces + IBM Plex Mono instead of the
  Cardo + PT Mono pairing Calyx itself uses, so the build shares Calyx's structural logic
  without repeating its palette or its faces.
- **Grammar:** gallery/catalog (full-bleed photographic hero, then a ledger-centred
  scrolling column, closing on one dark address-and-hours panel), chosen because the
  brief's own section list (a priced seasonal index, a subscription, a workshop
  cadence) is a catalogue's content, not a chaptered narrative's.
