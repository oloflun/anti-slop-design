## Why it works

The hero is framed by two large abstract planetary spheres — one dark
teal-black, one warm red-orange — sitting at opposite corners, mostly cropped
off-screen. They're pure illustration, no literal astronomy, but the product is
named "Comet," so the spheres function as a visual pun that also happens to
frame the central content in negative space without needing a bounding box.

A serif headline ("The browser that works for you") on plain cream ground
keeps the tone calm and editorial rather than technical, which matters for a
browser product: the pitch is meant to feel like a considered upgrade to
something familiar, not a new category to learn. The parent brand is credited
in a small line above the headline ("A new browser from Perplexity"),
borrowing existing trust rather than asking the product to build its own from
zero.

Below the fold, four capabilities ("AI that understands," "AI that builds,"
"AI that emails," "AI that creates") are shown as a 2x2 grid of soft pastel
cards, each with a distinct, real UI moment inside — a summarize/ask/translate
panel, a chat building a website, a draft-email composer. Repeating the
sentence structure across four card titles turns a feature list into a
readable refrain, while each card's actual screenshot content stays specific.

## What to borrow (principle)

- **Use two abstract shapes to frame the hero in negative space**, cropped at
  opposite corners, rather than a bounding box or background fill — especially
  when the shapes can pun on the product name.
- **Credit a trusted parent brand in a small line above the headline** for a
  new spin-out product, to borrow credibility before the product has its own.
- **Repeat a sentence structure across feature-card titles** ("AI that
  understands / builds / emails / creates") so a feature grid reads as a
  refrain rather than a disconnected list.
- **Keep the download CTA plain and platform-explicit** (a pill listing Mac,
  Windows, iOS, Android) rather than a vague "get started."

## What to leave

- Two large illustrated spheres this abstract risk reading as decorative rather
  than meaningful if the "Comet" name/pun isn't reinforced anywhere else on the
  page.
- A 2x2 feature grid with four near-identical pastel card treatments can
  under-differentiate genuinely different capabilities (summarizing vs.
  building vs. emailing) if the only variation is the screenshot inside.
