## Why it works

The studio identity is built almost entirely from two colours — a flat olive-green field
and a single chartreuse-yellow ink — applied with total consistency across the homepage
and the project pages. Because there is no white, no black and no photography in the
identity screen itself, the custom rounded wordmark and its three hand-drawn icons (a
play triangle, a sun-burst, a star) read as a logotype system rather than a decorated
headline; each icon reappears as a bullet before its matching service label in the row
beneath, so the mark and the navigation share one vocabulary.

Project pages invert the balance: the same olive-and-chartreuse frame recedes to a border,
and full-bleed documentary sports photography (shot on location, natural light, real
motion) fills nearly the entire viewport. A single letter of the studio name sits in each
corner of the frame — "P" top-left, "A" top-right, continuing down the page — so the
brand mark is present on every plate without ever occupying space a photograph could use.

The typography stays uniform throughout: one tracked small-caps sans (Annuario) sets every
label, from top-level nav to photo captions to the project title's supporting line,
leaving the custom wordmark as the only display-scale exception anywhere in the system.
That restraint is what lets the photography, not typography, carry the case-study pages.
The copy matches: headings run in tight sentence case and a neutral, third-person voice,
so the wordmark and colour system carry the studio's personality rather than the language
(from designlang extract).

## What to borrow (principle)

- **Build a wordmark from the same colour pair used everywhere else**, so the logo reads
  as generated by the system rather than pasted on top of it.
- **Turn service icons into both logo ornaments and navigation bullets.** Reusing the same
  three marks in two contexts teaches the vocabulary once.
- **Mark the brand with corner tags on full-bleed photography instead of a persistent
  header/footer chrome.** The photo keeps the whole frame; the brand still reads on every
  screen.
- **Hold every label to one tracked small-caps sans.** A single display exception (the
  wordmark) stays legible as an exception only if nothing else competes with it.

## What to leave

- Splitting the wordmark across four screen corners (P / A / Y / A) depends on the viewer
  reading top-to-bottom, left-to-right in the right order — it works as a subtle signature
  but wouldn't survive as a primary way to communicate the brand name to a first-time
  visitor.
- The homepage's icon-labelled service row sits in a large field of otherwise empty
  olive ground; on a wider viewport this risks reading as unfinished rather than
  intentionally spacious.

## Signature move

The four-corner letter tag: instead of a persistent logo lockup, a single character of the
studio's name sits in each corner of a full-bleed project photograph, turning every
image plate into a piece of branded stationery without ever adding a bar, rule or watermark
across the photo itself.

## Motion

Overall feel reads as springy on a scroll-linked page — the one named easing family the
extract found is a genuine spring/overshoot curve, `cubic-bezier(0.56, 0.56, 0.4, 1.13)`,
whose >1 control point gives it a slight bounce past its resting value. Duration steps run
`md` 300ms and `lg` 500ms, and the spring curve is paired with a 0.5s transition (the
generic `transform 0.4s` and unitless `0.3s` transitions elsewhere stay on the browser's
default ease). The keyframes are a coordinated loading-screen family rather than
content-reveal animation: a plain `spin`, a centred `loading-default` rotation, a
box-shadow-driven `loading-bars` bounce (bars grow at the 40% mark), and two orbiting
dot-trail loaders (`loading-trail-1`, `loading-trail-2`) that sweep a ring of dots around a
centre point. Together they read as a branded pre-loader shown before the page settles,
not as scroll-triggered choreography on the icons or wordmark — no stagger pattern for the
page content itself is documented. (from designlang extract)

## Brief

Build a production-studio identity on a flat two-colour field (deep olive ground, single
chartreuse-yellow ink, no white or black in the core palette). Open the homepage on a
centred custom rounded wordmark accompanied by two or three small hand-drawn icons, and
repeat those same icons as bullets in a service-label row beneath. Hold every label, nav
item and caption to one tracked small-caps sans; reserve display scale for the wordmark
alone. For project/case-study pages, run full-bleed documentary photography with minimal
UI chrome — replace a persistent header/footer with a single letter of the studio name
placed in each corner of the frame, spelling the name out as the visitor scrolls through
the piece.
