## Why it works

The hero is four bitten-open protein bars, shot on black, cropped so the torn
wrapper and the chocolate coating fill the frame — hunger photography, not
nutrition-label photography. Each wrapper carries its own flavour-coded colour
band (blue, plum, red, cream) against a shared gold-foil base, so the product
range reads as a set without needing a separate "flavours" grid.

The commercial structure is stated as a tier system — "David Gold is
disciplined. David Bronze is desired. Have it all." — which turns a bulk-
discount offer ("buy 4 cartons, get 1 free") into a small piece of brand
mythology instead of a plain coupon. Tiering the product line by metaphor
(gold/bronze) rather than by size or flavour gives the brand something to say
beyond price.

A scrolling ticker of proof points (`19,000+ positive reviews`, `free shipping
over $50`, `satisfaction guarantee`) runs continuously beneath the hero, doing
the reassurance work in peripheral vision rather than in a dedicated trust
section. The shop-all grid that follows keeps every tile on the same black
studio ground as the hero, so the page never breaks its own photographic
register moving from hero to catalogue.

## What to borrow (principle)

- **Shoot the product bitten or torn open**, not sealed — showing the eating
  moment sells appetite, not packaging.
- **Give a value-tier system a brand name** (Gold/Bronze) instead of presenting a
  bulk discount as a plain percentage-off offer.
- **Run trust proof as a continuous ticker**, not a static badge row, so it reads
  as ambient credibility rather than a claim to verify.
- **Keep hero and catalogue photography on the same ground colour** so the page
  doesn't visually reset moving from pitch to shop.

## What to leave

- A ticker this dense, repeating four or five claims on loop, risks becoming
  visual noise if it runs faster than a comfortable reading pace.
- Naming products after precious-metal tiers (Gold/Bronze) only works if the
  actual product difference (formulation, price) is legible nearby — otherwise
  it reads as branding without substance.

## Signature move

The tier line as closing copy: "David Gold is disciplined. David Bronze is
desired. Have it all." Three short sentences that turn a subscribe-and-save
upsell into a piece of brand voice, placed right next to the bundle-builder CTA
rather than in a hero.

## Brief

Build a black-ground, studio-lit consumer packshot page for a tiered snack
product line. Open on 3-4 bitten or torn product shots on black, each wrapper
carrying its own accent colour against a shared metallic base colour, with a
single "most value" claim and a primary CTA. Run a continuous proof ticker
(review count, shipping threshold, guarantee) directly beneath the hero. Follow
with a dense shop-all grid of studio product photography on the same black
ground. Close on a bundle-builder module paired with one lifestyle bite-shot
photo and a short, tiered brand-voice line.
