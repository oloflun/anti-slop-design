## Why it works

The hero is competent, generic-good: a real modern house photographed at dusk,
warm interior light glowing against a cooling sky, a plain two-line claim
("Harmony shaped by light, material, and space") and a small "[SCROLL DOWN]"
cue. Nothing here is wrong — it's the correct recipe for an architecture-studio
hero, warm light against cool sky, plain confident copy, a registered wordmark.

The interesting failure shows up in the "about" copy just below the fold, which
drifts, mid-paragraph, from architecture language into unrelated software
language: "every detail we design — from a color system to a dashboard
layout — is rooted in user behavior..." A studio it claims to have "founded
with a vision to craft spaces" is, one sentence later, talking about ensuring
"every product scales beautifully, performs seamlessly across platforms." This
is copy written for a SaaS product, pasted into an architecture template
without being reread — a genuine tell of templated or AI-generated content that
never got a human edit pass.

Everything else on the page follows the category's honest defaults: a paired
interior-photo-plus-portrait "about" block, and a four-stat achievement row
(`95+ Curated Interiors`, `7 Years of Expertise`...). It's a useful reference
precisely because it shows how far competent visual defaults can carry a page
even when the copy underneath them has stopped meaning anything specific.

## What to preserve

- **Photograph real work at dusk**, warm interior light against a cooling sky —
  it's a reliable, flattering register for architecture and interior work.
- **Pair a portrait of the designer with an interior photograph** in the "about"
  block, so the studio has a human face, not just a body of work.
- **State achievement stats as a plain four-number row** directly after the
  "about" copy, as concrete follow-through on the claim just made.

## What to leave

- Copy that drifts from the stated category (architecture) into unrelated
  vocabulary (color systems, dashboards, "scales beautifully... across
  platforms") within the same paragraph — this is the single clearest tell
  that content was templated or generated without a domain-specific edit pass,
  and it undermines every other well-executed choice on the page.
- A registered-trademark symbol on a studio wordmark with no other markers of
  an established, litigation-aware brand reads as borrowed gravitas rather than
  earned.

## Signature move

Not a device worth reusing — the notable "move" here is a cautionary one: the
about-section copy silently swaps industries mid-paragraph, moving from spatial
design language to software-product language without the switch being
acknowledged or corrected. It's the page's most instructive feature precisely
because it's a mistake.

## Brief

If borrowing this direction: keep the dusk architectural hero, the paired
portrait-plus-interior "about" block, and the four-stat achievement row — all
genuinely solid defaults for the category. Replace the about-section copy
entirely with language specific to the actual practice (materials, process,
typology, geography) and have it read by someone who would notice a stray
"dashboard layout" or "scales across platforms" reference before it ships.
Never let generic or copy-pasted template language survive into a page whose
whole pitch is bespoke, considered craft.
