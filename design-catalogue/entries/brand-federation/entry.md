## Why it works

The hero is a single abstract graphic — a folded, ribbon-like gradient shape in
deep red fading to bright orange — filling the entire viewport behind a plain,
conversational headline ("Turn your brand into a force for growth"). No
photography, no icon set, just one continuous abstract form doing all the
visual work, which suits a brand-strategy consultancy: the graphic reads as
"transformation" or "momentum" without depicting anything literal that could
date or narrow the pitch.

The service structure below is a clean triad — Research, Strategy,
Implementation — given three panels in three different tones (light grey, dark
navy, warm peach), each with its own bulleted capability list and a small
circular orange arrow button. Alternating panel tone rather than panel content
gives a plain three-column list visual rhythm without needing three different
illustrations.

The page's confidence comes from restraint in a specific place: everything
below the gradient hero is plain, functional, almost administrative — bulleted
lists in a single sans, no further photography or illustration — which lets
the one abstract gradient hero stand as the entire "creative capability" proof,
with the rest of the page reading as organised, credible delivery.

## What to borrow (principle)

- **Fill the entire hero with one abstract gradient graphic** rather than
  photography or icons, when the service (strategy, transformation) resists
  literal depiction.
- **Alternate panel background tone, not panel content structure**, to give a
  plain three-column service list visual rhythm.
- **Reserve your one accent colour for small circular action buttons** across an
  otherwise neutral service section, so it stays a clear "click here" signal.
- **Let one bold visual statement (the hero) carry all the creative weight**,
  and let everything after it be plain and organised — the contrast itself
  reads as competence.

## What to leave

- A saturated red-to-orange gradient this large risks reading as generic
  "AI startup" visual shorthand if it isn't paired with a genuinely distinct
  headline — the words need to be doing real work since the graphic alone
  won't differentiate the brand.
- Three alternating panel tones (light, dark, peach) in one row can create
  uneven perceived hierarchy — the dark-navy centre panel visually dominates
  even if "Strategy" isn't meant to outweigh "Research" or "Implementation."

## Brief

Build a plain, credibility-first consultancy page anchored by one abstract
gradient hero. Fill the entire hero viewport with a single continuous abstract
shape (a folded ribbon, a fluid gradient) in two related warm hues, with a
plain conversational headline in white set over it and one CTA. Drop to white
for the service structure: three panels in alternating tones (light, dark,
warm) each naming one service phase with a short bulleted capability list and
a small circular accent-coloured arrow button. Keep all type in a single plain
sans throughout, no further illustration after the hero.

## Image recipe

Not applicable — this reference is carried by an abstract gradient/ribbon
graphic rather than photography. If photography is substituted, use a single
abstract, non-literal texture (fabric, liquid, light trails) in the brand's two
hero colours, filling the full viewport with no recognisable subject.
